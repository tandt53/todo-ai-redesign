## Summary
AC-27 "has a shipped failure record and it is worth reading as a list of what to test: the original product's generator silently lost the successor's **list**, its **reminder** and its **sub-tasks** — three separate bugs, all found at once, all invisible because each occurrence looked like a perfectly ordinary task." A fourth is this repo's to avoid: theirs placed the successor by reading the completed row's own collection. All four are asserted, plus the all-day anchor product F2 found.

## Preconditions
- In-process harness: `createApp(deps)` with the `__qa__` doors mounted in front, on an ephemeral port, driven by supertest. Fresh durable store per test.
- The clock seam and the account zone are both held at `T0 = 2026-08-19T12:00:00.000Z` / `UTC` by `POST /__qa__/set-clock` (AC-44). Every fixture instant is derived from `T0`, never from a wall clock (L-023).
- A repeating occurrence carrying a note, a priority, a reminder at a known offset, and N steps — one of them hand-ticked, so the successor's steps must come back clean regardless.

## Test steps (API)
| # | Case (automation `it` name) | Request | Expected |
|---|------------------------------|---------|----------|
| 1 | TC-19a the completed occurrence stays as HISTORY and exactly one successor appears | complete a weekly occurrence | the completed row keeps `deleted_at: null`; exactly one successor, dated the next Monday, carried in `changed` |
| 2 | TC-19b generation happens inside the completing write | complete, then `POST /__qa__/reopen-store` | one request returned both rows, and both are on disk after that single write |
| 3 | TC-19c the successor carries note, priority and EVERY step, all unticked and unclaimed | 3 steps, one hand-ticked, then complete | note and priority copied; three steps, all not-done, `completed_by_parent: false`, `reminder_shown_at: null`, distinct positions |
| 4 | TC-19d the reminder travels by OFFSET from the due | complete an occurrence whose reminder is 24h before the due | the successor's reminder is NOT the old instant, and the due−reminder offset is identical |
| 5 | TC-19e reminder_shown_at is never inherited | acknowledge the reminder, then complete | the successor's marker is `null` |
| 6 | TC-19f on an ALL-DAY due the offset is whole days and keeps its wall-clock time | all-day due, reminder the previous day at 17:30, daily repeat | successor all-day, reminder at 17:30 the next day; the offset is a whole number of days |
| 7 | TC-19g the successor's placement follows the ROLLED DUE | complete an `archived` occurrence | the successor is dated from the roll and inherits neither `done` nor `archived` |

## Expected behaviour
- Exactly one successor per occurrence, carried in the write's own response (AC-26's multi-row rule) — "a successor generated by a hand tick appears **nowhere** until some unrelated event refreshes" without it.
- Every step travels, **all unticked and with `completed_by_parent` clear**, so a cascade on the new occurrence reverses correctly (AC-27).
- The reminder keeps its **offset**, not its instant: "an alert copied verbatim onto next month's task is already in the past."
- On an all-day due the offset is whole days and the reminder keeps its own wall-clock time — never measured from a fabricated local midnight, "the anchor an implementer reaches for and exactly what AC-13 exists to prevent. Invisible on the first occurrence; it appears on the roll."

## Notes
- *Would this notice?* Yes — a verbatim reminder copy fails TC-19d; a midnight-anchored offset fails TC-19f's hour/minute assertions; a collection-derived placement fails TC-19g; a generator that dropped steps fails TC-19c's `toHaveLength`.
- TC-19b's reopen is what turns "inside the transaction" from an unobservable claim into a durable one at this tier.
