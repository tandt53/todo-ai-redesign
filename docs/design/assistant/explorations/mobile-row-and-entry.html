Inline "Add a task" sits in the list. The FAB violates DESIGN.md line 86: "iOS has no FAB." On iOS this would be a header button instead, splitting voice entry from text entry.

Recommendation: B

Drop the time rail on phone. Keep the bottom text field with mic. The time rail is the differentiator and it works on tablet and desktop where it has room. On a 390px phone it costs 18.5% of every row for a column that is sometimes "—". The title is the one thing a todo list is made of, and untimed rows in B get 96px more for it than in A.

What each option costs

OptionGainsCosts
A No rule changes. The time rail stays on every surface. 72px (18.5%) reserved on every row. Untimed tasks get 96px less title width than B. The "—" reads as missing data, not as "no time." Timed and untimed rows look different enough that the owner already sees them as two UIs.
B Title gets the full row width on untimed tasks. Timed and untimed rows look the same except for the quiet due-date text on the right. Bottom field + mic is one unified entry point, and the most familiar pattern (Apple Reminders, Todoist, Microsoft To-Do). The time rail differentiator is tablet/desktop only. The placement conflict exists: the text field is at the bottom but a new task lands at its sorted position mid-list. Solved by scroll-to-new + highlight (the NEW tag and accent bar shown above). Swipe-to-delete needs a second accessible path.
C Inline row is IN the list, so the spatial gap between entry and result is smaller. The FAB violates DESIGN.md line 86 ("iOS has no FAB"). On iOS the voice button would be a header action, splitting voice and text entry into two different locations. Two entry mechanisms vs. one. The inline row takes a permanent row in the list.

Rule changes B requires

  • DESIGN.md line 88 "Row delete control ... always visible" changes to swipe-to-reveal on mobile. The second path for VoiceOver and TalkBack: the task detail screen (tap the row) already has a Delete button. A custom-action rotor entry for Delete also works. Both are standard iOS/Android patterns. Swipe without a second path is a lockout, not a gesture.
  • DESIGN.md ## Identity, differentiator narrows from "every task row" to "tablet and desktop only." The time rail is still the signature element; it just does not appear on the one surface where it cannot breathe. No line in DESIGN.md says the rail must appear at every breakpoint, so this is an explicit scope note rather than a contradiction.

What I could not settle by drawing

  • Real ratio of timed vs. untimed tasks. The fixture is 14/18 (78% timed), but that is authored data. If real users have 40% untimed tasks, the "—" penalty is larger than it looks. If 95% are timed, the rail is earning its space. This is answerable only by the owner's own usage or by telemetry.
  • Whether scroll-to-new is enough on a long list. A list of 30 tasks where the new one appears 20 rows above the text field is a bigger spatial gap than the 6-task prototype shows. The highlight and scroll help, but long lists may want a brief toast ("Added above") as well.
