Two of v1's five colours are gone. green / success — the label NEW was already doing the work, because state is never allowed to travel on colour alone. The new value now reads bold against the old one struck through and muted, which a colour-blind reader can follow by construction.

The cyan → violet voice pair — it split you are speaking from the assistant is thinking across two hues plus a gradient handoff. The gradient is not legal in this direction, the handoff never once rendered in the running app, and who is speaking is already carried by side, ground and label. One accent now means the assistant, on both halves of its turn.

The fill and the outline of a red button are not the same button. danger filled is the action that does the deleting — it appears in a confirmation and nowhere else. danger outlined is the action that asks. That is why Delete task above is quiet and Delete is not.

Why one family. The direction locks a single grotesque for both display and body. Product copy is English, but the last row above is the test that decides it: a task title is whatever the user types, and the live store holds that one today. A display face picked for English headings breaks first on stacked diacritics, at exactly the size the app uses most. So hierarchy is carried by size, weight, case and tracking instead, and the second face is reserved for numerals only.

Two floors, neither negotiable. Body never below 16 and line-height never below 1.5 — under 16 iOS zooms the page on focus and tone marks lose their separation from the letter. iOS lifts body to 17; Android sets meta to 14. Nothing else in the ramp diverges.

The time rail is structure, not metadata. The time sits first, right-aligned, set in the numeric face, with a 1px rule running the whole length of the list. A task with no time shows —, so the zero case is drawn rather than left blank. The column steps 72 → 96 → 136 with width and picks up the weekday at the widest, which is what a wide screen buys instead of a gutter.

One signal per meaning. The Overdue heading carries the red; the times in its rows stay muted. The priority mark is one !, at least space.2 clear of the title — v1 computed margin-left: 0 and put !!! hard against the last letter of the task's own name.

A title is never truncated to protect a column. It wraps to two lines and the row grows. Switch to 390 above to see it: the rail keeps its place and the name stays readable. Clipping a task's own name to keep a column tidy is the same mistake the 197px field made in plate 06.

What moves

Exactly one thing on screen moves on its own: a 2px accent rule running along the composer while the microphone is live. It replaces v1's 2400ms aurora breath, and it is the entire motion budget of the system.

A change is stated field by field. The old value is struck through and muted, the new one is bold — no colour needed. Undo always carries its word; it is never an icon on its own.

Undo is the accent; Put back is not. Undo reverses what the assistant did, so it wears the assistant's colour. Putting back something your own hand deleted is a neutral button, because it was never the assistant's act.

A confirmation names what it will delete — up to three titles, then “and N more”. A question that states only a count is one a user cannot answer safely.

prefers-reduced-motion: every animation collapses to one 80ms opacity change, and every end state is kept.

06

Fields, labels and form rows

Defined before anyone draws a form. v1 never defined them, and the task detail shipped without them.

frame 1440pxthe Tasks pane only — a form row is min(100%, 640)
The whole name fits. A field has no width of its own — it is always 100% of its form row.
List name can't be empty.

The defect this rule blocks

v1 — not allowed

A 197px input inside a pane with 823px to spare, clipping the last word of the task's own title. The class was used in JSX and styled nowhere, so the input fell back to the HTML default size=20.

The rule

A field has no intrinsic width. Always width:100% of its form row. The size attribute and any fixed px width on an input are forbidden.

Checkable: inside a form row wider than 320, a field rendering below 240px is a failure.

A label is always present and always above the field. A placeholder is never a label — it disappears exactly when the reader needs it.

A form row is min(100%, 640px) wide with 24px between rows. An error sits directly under its field, at meta size, in danger, with the border turning too — the one place two signals are allowed, because a form error the reader misses costs them the whole form.

One centring, and it is at the content column. Each frame column holds exactly one content column, min(pane − 2×gutter, its measure), centred in its pane. Everything inside it is left-aligned and nothing centres again. That is the single line v1 was missing: .tasks-col was max-width: 720px with no margin: 0 auto.

Two checks follow, and together they are the underflow test that has never existed here. First: for every frame column, measure the leftover on each side of its content column — differ by more than 8px, fail. That is what turns the red row above into a reportable defect. Second: a column's content must cover 90% of its inner width unless a declared measure token explains the shortfall. A capped measure with even leftover is correct; a column that stopped growing with nothing behind it is not.

design-check tests horizontal overflow and nothing else, which is how it reported 60 of 60 passing with 780px of nothing on screen.

breakpoints now declares seven widths, up to 1920. v1 declared four and stopped at 1280 — which is why its worst defect was invisible to every check that ran, and present in its own mockup.

Why the rail arrives at 1536 and not at 1440. 1536 is the narrowest frame where the rail fits without the Tasks list ending up narrower than it was one breakpoint below — 240 rail + 820 list + 2 gutters + 420 Talk. Bring it in at 1440 and a user maximising their window watches the list shrink, which is a regression wearing a feature's clothes.

The Lists rail also answers a second finding. v1 showed a 320px phone drawer, holding five rows and roughly 700px of nothing, on a 1440px screen — and dimmed the Talk panel behind it while it was open.
